The Magazine St Food Tours

The gator tour is booked. Everything here walks Magazine St or the Garden District — that rule covers every section, and the French Quarter is off the page. Two food tours at $376 and $426 for the four of you, and one cocktail tour at $249 for the three of you, because no cocktail tour in this city will take a 7-year-old.

🍽️ 1. Food tours

Priced for 3 adults and a 7-year-old, cheapest first. Both walk Magazine St, which is the whole point: the two operators that only run the French Quarter were dropped. Doctor Gumbo has six tours and every one of them is in the Quarter; Destination Kitchen does go to the Garden District, but only on a 21+ cocktail-and-architecture walk, not a food tour. Stops below are each operator's own published list — one names them, one refuses, and the card says which.

What the $39 seat actually is
🧒 “Reduced – Non Eater” · phone only
Their booking system does not call it a child ticket. It is a non-eater fare, and it exists on every departure — it is simply never shown to an online buyer.
◆ Their own words: “Children 12 and under who share portions with a parent qualify for a discounted ticket at $39, available by calling our office directly. If your child is a big eater, a full-priced ticket may be the better call.”
◆ So it is a sharing fare, not a discount. He eats off your plates for $39, or eats his own for $129. Worth deciding before you dial, because the $90 gap is the whole saving on this page.
◆ The other seats: $129 is the non-refundable ticket — no refund, no change. $149 buys a full refund up to 24 hours out. That is $20 a head for a cancellable trip.

🍸 3. Cocktail tours

Same rule as the food tours — Magazine St or the Garden District, one tour per operator — plus one more: he has to be able to come. Only one cocktail tour walks the Garden District, and no cocktail tour in this city will take a 7-year-old. Five operators’ own FAQs checked on 4 Sept 2026, and every one of them is 21+. Priced for three adults, with the discounts each one keeps off its website in the last card.

Nobody discounts a non-drinker
🚫 No DD rate, no dry ticket, anywhere
You asked whether any of them let a non-drinker off the fare. Checked on all five: none does. What differs is whether the drinks are in the price at all.
◆ The closest thing is a tour that does not include drinks. Secrets and Sidewalk both charge $39 for the guiding only — you buy your own at each stop, so a non-drinker pays $39 and nothing after that. Sidewalk says it outright: “Some people will want to enjoy cocktails, others will not. This tour is designed to accommodate either choice.”
◆ The Garden District one runs the other way. Its $83 has a cocktail built in, so a non-drinker is paying for a drink they will not have — the one case here where being dry costs you money.
◆ Doctor Gumbo swaps rather than discounts: a mocktail in place of each cocktail, same $95.

Deals
What is worth asking for on the phone
📞 Two real discounts exist, neither sold online
Same hunt as the food tour, run over all four operators’ booking systems on 4 Sept 2026. No coupon site has anything, and no checkout here has a promo box — but two genuine rates turned up sitting behind a phone call.
◆ Destination Kitchen keeps a “2 for 1 – Venue App” rate on this exact tour. It is in their booking system alongside the normal ticket, and it is not sellable online. VenueApp is a free New Orleans app — no invitation, no host code — and its own release notes mention limiting “2 for 1 events to one ticket”. If it applies to your date, three seats become two: $249 down to $166. Worth the question, not worth counting on.
◆ Doctor Gumbo runs an “Adult (No drinks included)” rate — the non-drinker fare you were after. It is in their rate table and it is also not sold online; the public page offers one $95 adult ticket and nothing else. Their tour is French Quarter, so it is out on location, but if that changes, the dry seat exists and you have to ask for it.
◆ Three cannot book the Garden District tour online at all. The quantity box on the 10:30 AM departure starts at six, and the 1:30 PM one is phone-only every single day. Ring 504-226-5500; the room holds 13, so space is not the problem.
◆ No promo codes exist on any of them. Destination Kitchen and New Orleans Secrets both book through FareHarbor with discount codes switched off, so there is no field to type one into.
◆ The $83 is genuine, for once. Their site strikes $88 down to $83 and the checkout really does charge $83 — unlike the “$75” that NOLA Adventures still advertises on the food tour.
◆ Private is not a saving at this size. New Orleans Secrets will sell their cocktail tour privately at $399 for up to ten, which only beats their $39 a head at nine people or more. For three it is $117 the normal way, or $147 if you want the refundable ticket.
💡 4. Before you book
  • 🧒 Both have a child rate — that is new. Sidewalk charges $79 for ages 4–17 and nothing under 4; New Orleans Secrets does $39 for under-12s sharing portions, by phone on 504-517-5397, not online. The two French Quarter tours that came off this page charged him a full adult fare, so the shortlist got cheaper per head as it got shorter.
  • 💵 $50 apart, and the gap is the group size. $376 against $426. Sidewalk is a standard walk with five stops; Secrets caps at nine guests, covers gratuities and taxes, pours a beer, and serves full portions rather than tastes.
  • 📉 Read the review counts, not just the stars. 4.7 on 26 reviews is a thinner promise than 4.9 on 304. Sidewalk answers that by naming its venues — its six carry about 3,500 Yelp reviews between them, which is the number to weigh.
  • 🏷️ Groupon reaches neither of them. New Orleans Secrets is on Groupon, but only with its $49 French Quarter history walk — the Magazine food tour is not sold there. Sidewalk’s $89 was compared against a $79 direct price that turns out to be the child fare, and the adult fare is $99, so that one is worth re-pricing before you book.
