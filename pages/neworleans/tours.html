✓ Verified 2026-08-22 — how to read the prices on this page

A price and a coupon expire, and the expiry is not printed on them. Every deal in this section carries the date it was checked and the page it was checked against, so a reader can tell a fresh number from a stale one. Cards elsewhere on this page were written in one pass in August 2026 and carry only a blanket footer claim — treat those as undated.

These dates are day-granular, and that is a deliberate limit rather than an omission. A stamp reading 2026-08-22 means someone opened the seller's page that day. It does not mean the price held all day — an operator can change a fare or pull a promotion between breakfast and dinner, and a day-granular stamp cannot express that and does not try to. So: a same-day stamp is worth trusting for which price was published; confirm the number itself at checkout, which is the only place it is ever authoritative. If you are reading this on a later date than the stamps, treat every figure as a starting point for a comparison, not as a quote.

Where an operator publishes a price directly, that is the number shown, because operator-direct is usually cheaper than the aggregator — and where it is not, that is said too. No promo code appears here unless it was found on the seller's own page. Discount mechanisms (free-tour-plus-tip, advance purchase, combo, city pass, kids-free ages) are preferred over codes: they rot far more slowly.

🚐 1. Getting to the swamp, and back, without a plantation

The swamp is the one thing on this page that is not walkable, so the ride is the decision and the boat is the detail. Every figure below is for 3 adults + one 7-year-old and excludes any plantation stop. Checked at each operator's own page on 2026-08-22.

🏨 Your hotel is on Airboat Adventures' printed pickup list, by name

Most operators say "downtown hotel pickup" and leave you to find out at booking whether that includes yours. Airboat Adventures publishes the actual roster, and "Dauphine Orleans, 415 Dauphine St, New Orleans, LA 70112" is on it — one of 37-plus Quarter and downtown hotels. The van comes to the door; there is no walk to a meeting point and no phone call needed to find out.

Take the covered pontoon, not the airboat. Airboat Adventures' own page draws the line: airboats are minimum age 5, and "the covered pontoon boat on the other hand, is suitable for all ages." Pontoon $35 adult / $15 youth 5–12, plus a flat $30 per person round trip for the van — $240 for the four of you, pickup window opening 8:30 AM for the 9:45 AM boat.

🚫 The "no Uber back from the bayou" warning is true, and the operators say so themselves

This one usually circulates as travel-blog folklore. It is not. Airboat Adventures, on its own site: "Uber and cab services are not available in this area so we make it easy for you to get here." New Orleans Kayak Swamp Tours is blunter: "PLEASE do not use Uber… our tour leaves from a roadside boat launch with no physical address. Also, rural Louisiana doesn't always have the best Uber service."

The outbound leg generally works. It is the return — from a rural launch with no street address, after the boat has docked — that fails, and you find out when it is already too late to fix. Uber's own route pages put New Orleans → Marrero at 12 miles / $28 average and Slidell → New Orleans at ~30 miles / ~$50, but the Marrero figure is to the town; the dock sits about nine miles further down Barataria Blvd. Read $90–120 round trip as a floor, and as a bet on a ride that may not come.

Boat tours cancel for weather, and Cajun Encounters is the strictest of the operators checked: bookings made inside 36 hours are final, with one courtesy reschedule. Leave a day behind it.
  • 🚗 A rental car costs the same as the shuttle, and buys you a driving day Avis and Budget share 1317 Canal St, 0.8 miles from the hotel, and both offer free customer pick-up with 24 hours' notice. But a booster is legally required for the 7-year-old in a rental (RS 32:295) at $14/day from Avis, the office shuts at 1 PM on Sunday, and the all-in day lands at roughly $210–250 — statistically identical to the shuttle, except somebody drives.
  • 👶 A tour van is exempt from the booster law; a rental car is not Louisiana RS 32:295 requires a belt-positioning booster and a rear seat for a child under 13 — but its own definition of "motor vehicle" excludes a commercial van or taxi. An operator's shuttle sits outside the requirement entirely. Uber and Lyft are TNCs, named nowhere in the statute; treat rideshare as the grey area it is.
  • 🅿️ Same-day return means you never pay to park Dauphine Orleans charges $50 per car per night for valet. Every swamp launch checked has free parking, so a car collected and returned the same day costs nothing to keep. If it has to stay overnight, the 939 Toulouse St lot is from $15, four minutes' walk, against $40 at most of the nearer garages.
🎯 2. How to actually pay less — the channels, and the ones that are dead

Twenty-two deal channels were opened and read on 2026-08-22, from Viator down to a visitor-bureau coupon PDF. Most of the famous ones are empty. Four things work, they do not combine, and the order you do them in decides how much you keep.

⚠️ The stacking rule: you get one lane, not all four

This is the part every coupon roundup leaves out, and it is why "stack a code, a portal and a card offer" does not work. Each channel voids the others by its own written terms:

Cashback portals void on any code they did not publish. Rakuten: "Use of coupon/promotional codes not listed on Rakuten may void Cash Back." TopCashback says the same about a "coupon code not displayed on TopCashback". So a code found anywhere else costs you the 8%.
Rakuten additionally voids on card-linked offers — its exclusion list names them explicitly, and an Amex Offer is a card-linked offer. TopCashback's exclusion list does not mention them. Same 8% rate, so use TopCashback if you are also using an Amex Offer.
Groupon is one code per order, by its own FAQ — there is no sitewide-plus-deal stack.
Viator's Lowest Price Guarantee cannot be combined with a Viator promotion, and will not honour a price you got through AAA, AARP, a membership programme or a daily deal.
Both portals void on gift-card-funded bookings. Which kills the discounted-gift-card idea entirely — see below.

Costs you money
Folklore #1
Discounted Viator gift cards lose you 6%
🚫 The arithmetic runs backwards
Buying tour credit at a discount on a gift-card marketplace sounds like free money. The Viator-powered card on CardCash is "up to 2% off" — and 2% is the ceiling, not the typical. Against that, both cashback portals explicitly void on gift-card-funded bookings: Rakuten excludes "purchases made with gift vouchers", TopCashback excludes "purchase or redemption of a Viator gift card".
◆ So the trade is: save 2%, forfeit 8%. Net loss of about 6% against simply clicking through a portal. The Expedia-powered hotel card at 4–4.5% is a better card and the same logic applies to it.
✓ Verified 2026-08-22 · cardcash.com plus both portals' exclusion lists.
Folklore #2
A private tour is not a discount for four people
🚫 But it is sometimes the only way in
Private tours get recommended as a group saving. They are a buy-out of the whole boat, so they do not break even until you fill it: four seats on Cajun Encounters' 10–12 passenger VIP boat run about $400, and a private buy-out runs roughly $1,000–1,200. ToursByLocals' private airboat is a flat $1,050.50 for one to six people — $263 each at four.
◆ Where private is the answer is as an age unlock, not a discount. Cajun Encounters' VIP small boat is 13+ "unless booked as a private tour", and Cajun Pride's three VIP T-Cajun products are all 13+. If someone wants the small quiet boat with a 7-year-old aboard, buying it out is the only route — and New Orleans Secrets' private food tour at $399 for up to ten is the one case where the private rate genuinely beats three individual tickets.
✓ Verified 2026-08-22 · operators' own pages and booking systems. Neither Cajun Encounters nor Airboat Adventures publishes a private minimum, so a real quote needs a phone call.
Folklore #3
The hotel concierge and the Decatur St tour office sell on commission
🚫 Convenience, never a discount
Dauphine Orleans' own site advertises a courtyard pool and a continental breakfast and says nothing about a concierge, a tour desk or a partner discount. The walkable storefront offices are real — swamptours.com at 620 Decatur, Cajun Encounters at 941 Decatur — but a commissioned reseller has no reason to undercut the operator, and none publishes a walk-up discount.
◆ The one genuine walk-up mechanism is the printed coupon presented at a ticket window — and note how those are written: "applies to adult rates only", "printed coupon required", "direct bookings only", "not valid with any other offers". A coupon worth $10 an adult is worth nothing on the child.
The coupon site listing $10 off Cajun Pride and Jean Lafitte, $5 off Gray Line and $3 off the School of Cooking publishes no expiry dates at all, and it is built by the same web shop as one of the resellers it links to. The visitor-bureau version of the same idea is worse: the PDF that still ranks first in search is the 2020/21 guide, and every coupon in it expired 31 Jan 2021 — including BIGEASY10 and OVG20, both still circulating.

🔀 One structural change worth knowing: the aggregators may now legally undercut each other

Viator's updated global supplier agreement took effect on 1 August 2026, and its own operator-facing page states that Viator and its partners have autonomy over the final retail price shown to consumers. In plain terms, channel partners may now openly discount experiences or run them as loss leaders — something they previously could only do quietly.

What that means for you: the same New Orleans swamp tour may be cheaper on Expedia Things To Do or Booking.com Attractions than on Viator itself, and that gap is now a deliberate commercial lever rather than an accident. Check more than one aggregator on the same product, and remember the Lowest Price Guarantee above lets you claim the gap back if you booked the dearer one. ✗ Not verified: the Expedia comparison itself — Expedia rate-limited every attempt with HTTP 429, so no Expedia price appears anywhere on this page.   • 🚫 NEVER take Uber/Lyft to the Swamp Rideshare to rural bayous (Slidell/Lafitte/Marrero) works going out, but drivers will NOT accept return rides back to New Orleans. Always select the official operator shuttle add-on.
