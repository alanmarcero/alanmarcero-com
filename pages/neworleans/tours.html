🎯 2. How to actually pay less — the channels, and the ones that are dead

Twenty-two deal channels were opened and read on 2026-08-22, from Viator down to a visitor-bureau coupon PDF. Most of the famous ones are empty. Four things work, they do not combine, and the order you do them in decides how much you keep.

⚠️ The stacking rule: you get one lane, not all four

This is the part every coupon roundup leaves out, and it is why "stack a code, a portal and a card offer" does not work. Each channel voids the others by its own written terms:

Cashback portals void on any code they did not publish. Rakuten: "Use of coupon/promotional codes not listed on Rakuten may void Cash Back." TopCashback says the same about a "coupon code not displayed on TopCashback". So a code found anywhere else costs you the 8%.
Rakuten additionally voids on card-linked offers — its exclusion list names them explicitly, and an Amex Offer is a card-linked offer. TopCashback's exclusion list does not mention them. Same 8% rate, so use TopCashback if you are also using an Amex Offer.
Groupon is one code per order, by its own FAQ — there is no sitewide-plus-deal stack.
Viator's Lowest Price Guarantee cannot be combined with a Viator promotion, and will not honour a price you got through AAA, AARP, a membership programme or a daily deal.
Both portals void on gift-card-funded bookings. Which kills the discounted-gift-card idea entirely — see below.

Biggest single lever
Channel #1
Check Amex Offers before you book anything on Viator
💳 Targeted, per card — you have to log in and look
A targeted Amex Offer pays $40 back on a single $250 Viator purchase, usable twice, up to $80, through 17 Sep 2026. On roughly $500 of tours that is 16% — double what any portal or code pays. The $250 must be met in one transaction, not aggregated, and the card must be enrolled before you book.
◆ Card-linked offer. Three adults on a swamp tour plus one food tour clears $250 twice over if you split the bookings deliberately rather than buying everything in one basket. This is the one case where bigger baskets are worse.

Channel #2
Viator's Lowest Price Guarantee replaces "call the operator to price-match"
🔁 Claimable up to 48 hours after the trip starts
The old advice on this page was to phone the operator and ask them to match an aggregator. You do not need to. Viator's own guarantee refunds the difference if you find the same product cheaper on any other website — including the operator's own — right up until 48 hours after your trip begins. Minimum purchase $20. You email priceguarantee@viator.com with the booking number and proof.
◆ This inverts the usual order of operations. Book on Viator, where you can also collect portal cashback and the Amex Offer, then claim the difference against the cheaper direct price afterwards — rather than booking direct and forfeiting both.

Folklore #1
Discounted Viator gift cards lose you 6%
🚫 The arithmetic runs backwards
Buying tour credit at a discount on a gift-card marketplace sounds like free money. The Viator-powered card on CardCash is "up to 2% off" — and 2% is the ceiling, not the typical. Against that, both cashback portals explicitly void on gift-card-funded bookings: Rakuten excludes "purchases made with gift vouchers", TopCashback excludes "purchase or redemption of a Viator gift card".
◆ So the trade is: save 2%, forfeit 8%. Net loss of about 6% against simply clicking through a portal. The Expedia-powered hotel card at 4–4.5% is a better card and the same logic applies to it.
✓ Verified 2026-08-22 · cardcash.com plus both portals' exclusion lists.
Folklore #3
The hotel concierge and the Decatur St tour office sell on commission
🚫 Convenience, never a discount
Dauphine Orleans' own site advertises a courtyard pool and a continental breakfast and says nothing about a concierge, a tour desk or a partner discount. The walkable storefront offices are real — swamptours.com at 620 Decatur, Cajun Encounters at 941 Decatur — but a commissioned reseller has no reason to undercut the operator, and none publishes a walk-up discount.
◆ The one genuine walk-up mechanism is the printed coupon presented at a ticket window — and note how those are written: "applies to adult rates only", "printed coupon required", "direct bookings only", "not valid with any other offers". A coupon worth $10 an adult is worth nothing on the child.
The coupon site listing $10 off Cajun Pride and Jean Lafitte, $5 off Gray Line and $3 off the School of Cooking publishes no expiry dates at all, and it is built by the same web shop as one of the resellers it links to. The visitor-bureau version of the same idea is worse: the PDF that still ranks first in search is the 2020/21 guide, and every coupon in it expired 31 Jan 2021 — including BIGEASY10 and OVG20, both still circulating.

🔀 One structural change worth knowing: the aggregators may now legally undercut each other

Viator's updated global supplier agreement took effect on 1 August 2026, and its own operator-facing page states that Viator and its partners have autonomy over the final retail price shown to consumers. In plain terms, channel partners may now openly discount experiences or run them as loss leaders — something they previously could only do quietly.

What that means for you: the same New Orleans swamp tour may be cheaper on Expedia Things To Do or Booking.com Attractions than on Viator itself, and that gap is now a deliberate commercial lever rather than an accident. Check more than one aggregator on the same product, and remember the Lowest Price Guarantee above lets you claim the gap back if you booked the dearer one. ✗ Not verified: the Expedia comparison itself — Expedia rate-limited every attempt with HTTP 429, so no Expedia price appears anywhere on this page. The naming of Expedia, Booking.com and Costco as the partners is a trade publication's characterisation, not Viator's own wording.

🎷 3. French Quarter Food & Culinary Walking Tours

Six walking food tours in the Quarter, priced for 2 adults and a 7-year-old. Read the two boxes below before picking one: the child policy moves the total far more than any discount does.

🧒 The thing that decides this: nobody has a child rate

Checked 28 Aug 2026 across the operators below. A French Quarter food tour is $79 to $85 a head, and for a seven-year-old the usual policy is the part that hurts: children 6 and up pay the full adult fare. Under-4s and under-6s go free on several tours, which is no use to us. So the honest sticker price for three of us is $237 to $255, and a good share of that buys a child an oyster he will not eat.

Nothing found gets round it. Every operator below was checked for a child rate and none publishes one, so the sticker price is the price. The one card on this page from an operator that does discount children is New Orleans Secrets, and their tour here is the Hidden Culinary progressive dinner, which is 21+ — no use to us at all. If you want to push on it, they are the outfit worth phoning.

The cheap alternative is not a food tour at all. The New Orleans School of Cooking demo is $40–45, seated, indoors, air-conditioned and roughly two hours, and a child watching a chef make gumbo and then eating it is an easier sell at 11 AM in September than three hours on foot between five stops.

🏷️ Food-tour coupon hunt, and what it came to
